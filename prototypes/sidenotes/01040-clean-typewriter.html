The human in the loop

A short comparison text about attention, judgment, and the systems we build around them.

We often describe automation as the removal of human intervention. Yet the most consequential systems still depend on a person somewhere inside the loop.1

The interesting question is not whether the human remains, but where judgment is placed. A person may approve an output, correct an exception, or absorb the uncertainty that the system cannot represent.2

Side notes offer a similar design problem. They should make context available without forcing every reader to leave the main argument.3

01The phrase comes from cybernetics: a system that includes human judgment inside its feedback cycle.

02Efficiency is not neutral here. It decides which forms of attention are counted and which are discarded.

03A side note should remain optional: available at the point of curiosity without becoming a gate to the argument.
